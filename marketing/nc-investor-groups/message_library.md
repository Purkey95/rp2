# Message Library — NC Investor Groups

**Before first use:** every template below must be cleared by Longhorn's marketing/compliance
team. Bracketed `[FIELDS]` are deliberate — do not fill in rates, points, terms, LTV/ARV
percentages, or turnaround times from memory. Use only figures compliance has approved in
writing.

**Standing rules for every post**
- Never post the same variant twice in the same week, in any group.
- Never post the same variant to two groups on the same day.
- Disclose the relationship. Every promotional post needs some form of
  "I work with / I'm a rep for Longhorn Investments."
- No "guaranteed," "no credit check," "approval in X hours," or "everyone qualifies."
- No unapproved numbers. If you cite a rate or term, it must be compliance-approved copy.
- Answer questions in comments as yourself, not as a brochure.

---

## Type A — Educational (no pitch)
Use anywhere, including no-solicitation groups. This is your workhorse: it builds
recognition so that Type C and D land when you use them.

**A1 — Attorney-state mechanics**
> A thing that catches new NC investors off guard: North Carolina is an attorney-close
> state. Your buyer can't just walk into a title company here. If you're wholesaling and
> your cash buyer has never closed in NC, get them connected to a closing attorney early —
> I've seen deals die at the finish line over this. Anyone have a go-to closing attorney
> in [MARKET] they'd recommend?

**A2 — Scope-of-work discipline**
> The rehab budget mistake I see most often on first flips isn't underestimating materials
> — it's leaving no contingency line at all. If your SOW has zero slack and the inspection
> turns up [COMMON ISSUE IN MARKET], the whole deal math changes. What contingency % is
> everyone running right now?

**A3 — Draw schedules explained**
> For anyone who hasn't done a rehab loan before: the money doesn't come as one lump sum.
> It's usually a purchase advance at closing, then rehab funds released in draws as work
> gets completed and inspected. Which means you need working capital to front the first
> phase. Plan for that gap before you're standing in the house.

**A4 — Exit before entry**
> Underwrite the exit before you fall in love with the entry. If your ARV comps are more
> than [X] months old or more than a mile out, you don't have comps — you have hope.

**A5 — Seasonality**
> Anyone else seeing [MARKET] inventory shift this quarter? Curious what other folks'
> days-on-market look like right now vs. six months ago.

## Type C — Deal-enabler
The highest-converting type. Frames funding as the solution to a problem the group
already has. Use in groups that allow vendor participation.

**C1**

> through options. I work with Longhorn Investments on investor loans (fix-and-flip,
> rental, new construction). No obligation, and if it's not a fit I'll tell you straight.
> DM or comment.

**C2**
> Wholesalers: if you've got a buyer who loves the deal but says they can't close in time,
> that's often a financing problem, not a buyer problem. I work with Longhorn Investments —
> happy to talk to your buyer directly so the deal doesn't fall apart. No cost to you.

**C3**
> Question for the group — what's the #1 thing that kills your deals? For most people I
> talk to it's either the funding timeline or the rehab budget. I work the lending side
> with Longhorn Investments, so I see the first one constantly. Curious what it is for you.

> or close to it and want a second set of eyes on the numbers, send it over. I work with
> Longhorn Investments on the lending side so I look at a lot of these.
