# Kids' E-Bike Selection Criteria

The screening criteria that actually decide whether a given machine is right
for a specific child. Applied in [[kids-ebike-shortlist-for-adam]].

Motor wattage is the spec most listings lead with and the least useful one for
a child under 100 lb. Four gates matter more, and they are gates — a machine
that fails one is out regardless of how well it scores elsewhere.

## Gate 1 — Category: pedal bike or mini dirt bike?

The single most confusing thing in this market is that "kids electric bike"
covers two unrelated vehicles:

| | **E-bike** | **Mini electric dirt bike** |
|---|---|---|
| Pedals | Integral, with a drivetrain | Absent, or a bolt-on accessory |
| Pedal assist (PAS) | Yes — motor helps you pedal | No — twist/thumb throttle only |
| Ridable unpowered | Yes | No |
| Legal on roads/greenways | Yes, if class-compliant | No — it is a motor vehicle |

A throttle-only machine with accessory pedals is **not** an e-bike. The pedals
on such a frame exist for foot placement and kicking along, not for propulsion,
because there is no gearing and no assist sensor behind them.

Decide the category from how the child will actually ride before comparing any
specs across the line.

## Gate 2 — Fit: seat height vs. inseam

The child must get **both feet flat on the ground** while seated, or they cannot
hold the bike up at a stop. So:

> **seat height ≤ inseam** (barefoot, measured book-to-crotch against a wall)

Rules of thumb that mislead here:

- **Rider height ranges in listings are marketing.** They are commonly
  optimistic by 3–5 inches at the low end.
- **"Seat-to-pedal height" is not seat height.** It answers leg extension, not
  whether the child can touch down. Look for *seat-to-floor*, and for its
  *minimum* if the post is adjustable.
- Standover clearance is a separate check and does not substitute for this one.

## Gate 3 — Weight the child can manage

Weight matters for catching a tipping bike, righting a dropped one, and
walking it. A useful ratio is bike weight as a fraction of body weight; under
roughly 60% is manageable for a confident child, and approaching 90% is not.

Weight is a real constraint but it is **downstream of fit** — a bike the child
cannot touch down on is unsafe at any weight.

## Gate 4 — Certification and speed governance

- **UL 2849** (full electrical system) or **UL 2271** (battery) certification is
  the main safeguard against battery fire, and is increasingly required by
  schools, HOAs, and some parks. Absence of a certification claim on a
  sub-$1,000 import is meaningful.
- **Ask whether the speed limiter is parent-lockable.** A "5 speed modes"
  machine where the child can raise the mode from the handlebar display is
  functionally a top-speed machine. A limiter behind a parental code or a
  physical key is a different product from a limiter in the rider's menu.

## Secondary signals

- **Listing consistency.** When a manufacturer's own pages disagree about top
  speed, seat height, or age range, that inconsistency is data about the
  company's quality control and about what support will be like.
- **Certification + class compliance travel together.** A machine that fits a
  legal e-bike class is usually also the one with real brakes, lights, and a
  certified pack.
